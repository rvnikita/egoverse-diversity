A diversity score ranks subsets. It does not tell you which subset to train on — maximise it blindly and you get the worst one. One axis, atypicality, drives all three: it inflates the diversity score, it concentrates failure demos 3.28×, and it makes the subset worse to train on (ρ = -0.66, p < 0.001).

The registry has no usable outcome labels (eval_success is a dataclass default on all 439,053 rows); these came from task-name suffixes on one lab's episodes. Nothing above uses them — the scores are pure geometry, and they still rank failures. Note the trap: dist_to_centroid and knn20_dist have near-identical AUROC but their top-32 differ 7 vs 19. AUROC alone would pick the wrong one.

Mechanism: failures genuinely sit further out — Vendi 2.356 vs 2.033±0.066 for the same number of successes, mean distance to the mode 0.0739 vs 0.0598, Mann-Whitney p = 4.5e-28.

What it fails at

Exact duplicates cannot raise the score (Δ -0.047) — that is provable and it holds here. But near-duplicates can. Perturb a clone by a cosine distance of 0.0094 — well under the 0.0424 that separates genuinely distinct episodes — and the score starts to inflate, reaching +0.661 at 0.125. The score is gameable by anyone who submits jittered copies. We measured the threshold rather than claiming there isn't one.

Method: 32 uniform frames per episode → DINOv2-base (768-d, self-supervised, no text tower) on a Modal L4 → mean-pooled per episode → Vendi Score (exponential of the Shannon entropy of the eigenvalues of the normalised cosine kernel). Audit classifier is PCA-16 + L2 logistic regression on a different representation (first 4 + last 4 frames), frozen a priori and identical across selectors. 20 seeds per selector per budget.
